Guía del profesor · Teacher guide (≈ 55 min)

Pacing guide: Assessment · All modes · Grammar: — · Product: Unit 1 IPA: interpretive reading + interpersonal conversation

  1. Instrucciones (3 min): Read the instructions aloud. Phones away. Students answer the exam MC on paper (numbers 1–10) or on their device; you reveal at the end only if you choose to.
  2. Interpretivo: la lectura (10 min): Students read silently; they may tap glossed words. Do not play the audio or show the translation.
  3. Interpretivo: preguntas (12 min): Ten exam MC items + one short written response in English or Spanish (your choice; English recommended for the inference question to assess comprehension, not production).
  4. Interpersonal: conversación (25 min): Pairs draw a card (unrehearsed). 3–4 minutes per pair while the rest of the class continues the written response or reads. Score with the interpersonal rubric. Alternatively, record pairs on devices.
  5. Reflexión (5 min): Students self-assess the unit Can-Dos.

IPA format per the pacing guide: interpretive reading + interpersonal conversation. Answer key (exam MC): 1 C · 2 A · 3 D · 4 B · 5 C · 6 A · 7 D · 8 B · 9 C · 10 B. Pair students of similar proficiency for the conversation; you may act as partner B for odd numbers. The polls are low-stakes strategy warm-ups; the reading poll only confirms the topic from the title, so run it before students see the questions.

Nothing typed on this page is saved or sent anywhere. Audio uses the computer's built-in Spanish voice.

Today you have two parts: (1) Interpretive: read a blog and answer 10 multiple-choice questions and one short question. (2) Interpersonal: talk with a partner for 3–4 minutes about a situation on a card. Work alone in part 1. Answers are not shown until the end.

Idea clave

Consejos:

  • No necesitas entender cada palabra. Busca las ideas principales.
  • Usa las palabras subrayadas (glosario) si las necesitas.
  • En la conversación: contesta, da detalles, reacciona y haz preguntas.

Tips: you don't need every word; use the glossary; in the conversation answer, add details, react and ask questions.

Entre dos mundos — el blog de Valentina

¡Hola a todos! Me llamo Valentina Reyes, tengo diecisiete años y vivo en Lawrence, Massachusetts. Nací aquí, pero mis padres son de Santiago de los Caballeros, en la República Dominicana. Por eso digo que vivo «entre dos mundos»: en casa hablamos español y comemos mangú los domingos, y en la escuela hablo inglés casi todo el día.

Soy una persona curiosa y bastante organizada, pero mi hermano mayor, Joel, dice que soy terca. ¡Tal vez tiene razón! Me llevo muy bien con mi mamá; en cambio, con Joel a veces discuto porque él siempre usa mi cargador. Me encanta la fotografía y me interesa mucho el periodismo. Lo que más me molesta es cuando la gente no escucha.

Este verano fue diferente. En julio mi familia y yo fuimos a Santiago para el cumpleaños número ochenta de mi abuela Altagracia. Estuvimos tres semanas en su casa. Al principio estuve un poco nerviosa porque mi español no es perfecto y mis primos hablan muy rápido. Pero ellos fueron muy pacientes conmigo y me enseñaron muchas expresiones nuevas.

Para la fiesta, toda la familia ayudó: mis tíos hicieron un sancocho enorme, mis primas pusieron flores en el patio y yo saqué cientos de fotos. Esa noche mi abuela me dijo algo que no voy a olvidar: «Mija, no importa dónde vivas, tus raíces siempre van contigo».

Ahora estoy otra vez en Lawrence y empecé mi tercer año de la escuela secundaria. Quiero crear un proyecto de fotografía sobre las familias dominicanas de mi ciudad. Para mí, ser de dos lugares no es un problema: es mi superpoder.

Hi everyone! My name is Valentina Reyes, I'm seventeen and I live in Lawrence, Massachusetts. I was born here, but my parents are from Santiago de los Caballeros, in the Dominican Republic. That's why I say I live «between two worlds»: at home we speak Spanish and eat mangú on Sundays, and at school I speak English almost all day.

I'm a curious and fairly organized person, but my older brother, Joel, says I'm stubborn. Maybe he's right! I get along very well with my mom; on the other hand, I sometimes argue with Joel because he always uses my charger. I love photography and I'm very interested in journalism. What bothers me most is when people don't listen.

This summer was different. In July my family and I went to Santiago for my grandmother Altagracia's eightieth birthday. We were at her house for three weeks. At first I was a bit nervous because my Spanish isn't perfect and my cousins talk very fast. But they were very patient with me and taught me lots of new expressions.

For the party, the whole family helped: my uncles made a huge sancocho, my cousins put flowers in the patio and I took hundreds of photos. That night my grandmother told me something I won't forget: «Honey, no matter where you live, your roots always go with you».

Now I'm back in Lawrence and I started my junior year of high school. I want to create a photography project about the Dominican families in my city. For me, being from two places isn't a problem: it's my superpower.
