Guía del profesor · Teacher guide (≈ 55 min)

Pacing guide: Assessment · All modes · Grammar: — · Product: Unit 3 IPA: interpretive reading + interpersonal conversation

  1. Instrucciones (3 min): Explain the three parts and timing. Phones away. The polls in each section are warm-ups only; do not reveal the reading answers until all students finish.
  2. Interpretivo (17 min): Students read silently (audio allowed once), answer the 8 MC items on paper or device. Reveal only after collecting answers, or the next day.
  3. Interpersonal (20 min): While the class writes (Part 3), call pairs to your desk. Draw a card; 3–4 minutes of conversation per pair; score each student with the rubric.
  4. Presentacional (in parallel): Students not in conversation write the 100-word paragraph. They must not use notes.
  5. Reflexión (3 min): Self-assessment scale at the end.

Al mar de espaldas

Me llamo Gabriela Rivera y vivo en Holyoke. Todos los veranos visito a mis abuelos en Puerto Rico, y este año llegué justo a tiempo para la Noche de San Juan, el 23 de junio. San Juan Bautista es el santo patrón de la capital, que lleva su nombre.

Esa tarde, mi familia se reunió en la playa de Isla Verde con comida, música y sillas de playa. Mi abuela trajo arroz con gandules y mi tío preparó pinchos en una parrilla pequeña. Había más de cien familias en la playa, y todos se saludaban como si fueran vecinos. Mis primos y yo nos pusimos ropa blanca, porque mi abuela dice que es la tradición de nuestra familia.

A medianoche, empezó la parte más emocionante: la gente se tira al mar de espaldas varias veces. Se dice que así se dejan atrás la mala suerte y los problemas del año. Mi abuelo, que tiene setenta y cinco años, se tiró al mar tantas veces como yo. ¡Y se divirtió más que todos!

Cuando era niña, celebraba el 4 de julio en Holyoke con mis padres. Veíamos los fuegos artificiales y comíamos hamburguesas. Las dos fiestas se celebran en verano y son tan familiares como divertidas. Sin embargo, el 4 de julio es más patriótico y ruidoso, mientras que la Noche de San Juan es más espiritual: es un momento para renovarse.

Ahora, para mí, la Noche de San Juan es la mejor noche del verano. El año que viene, quiero llevar a mis amigos de Holyoke para que vean cómo se celebra en la isla.

Haz clic en las palabras subrayadas para ver su significado.

My name is Gabriela Rivera and I live in Holyoke. Every summer I visit my grandparents in Puerto Rico, and this year I arrived just in time for the Night of San Juan, June 23. Saint John the Baptist is the patron saint of the capital, which bears his name.

That afternoon, my family got together at Isla Verde beach with food, music and beach chairs. My grandmother brought rice with pigeon peas and my uncle made skewers on a small grill. There were more than a hundred families on the beach, and everyone greeted each other as if they were neighbors. My cousins and I put on white clothes, because my grandmother says it's our family's tradition.

At midnight, the most exciting part began: people throw themselves backward into the sea several times. It's said that this way you leave behind the bad luck and problems of the year. My grandfather, who is seventy-five, threw himself into the sea as many times as I did. And he had more fun than anyone!

When I was a little girl, I celebrated the Fourth of July in Holyoke with my parents. We watched the fireworks and ate hamburgers. Both holidays are celebrated in summer and are as family-centered as they are fun. However, the Fourth of July is more patriotic and noisy, while the Night of San Juan is more spiritual: it's a moment to renew yourself.

Now, for me, the Night of San Juan is the best night of the summer. Next year, I want to bring my friends from Holyoke so they can see how it's celebrated on the island.

4Parte 3: Presentacional

Presentacional

Escribe un párrafo

Un estudiante de Colombia va a pasar un año en tu escuela. Escríbele sobre una celebración importante de tu comunidad o de tu familia. Explica cómo se celebra (usa el se), compárala con una fiesta del mundo hispanohablante y cuenta qué hiciste la última vez que la celebraste.

A student from Colombia is going to spend a year at your school. Write to them about an important celebration in your community or family. Explain how it is celebrated (use se), compare it with a holiday from the Spanish-speaking world and tell what you did the last time you celebrated it.
